What payment terms mean

TermsWhat it means
Due on receiptPay as soon as the invoice arrives.
Net 7, Net 14, Net 30Pay within 7, 14 or 30 calendar days of the invoice date.
Net 30 EOMPay within 30 days after the end of the month the invoice was dated in.
2/10 net 30Take 2% off if you pay within 10 days; otherwise pay the full amount within 30 days.

Write the exact due date on your invoice as well as the terms, such as "Due 19 November 2026 (Net 30)". A clear date leaves less room for argument than "Net 30" alone. Our free invoice generator has a box for it.

How to use this calculator

  1. Pick the invoice date, or press "Today".
  2. Enter the payment terms in days, or press a quick pick such as "Net 30". Tick EOM if the terms say "end of month".
  3. Offering an early payment discount? Enter the discount and the days, plus the invoice amount, to see the discount deadline and what the client would pay.

How it's calculated

Start date = invoice date (or the last day of that month, for EOM) Due date = start date + payment days Discount date = start date + discount days Yearly cost of skipping the discount = discount ÷ (100% − discount) × 365 ÷ (payment days − discount days)

The yearly cost shows how good a deal the discount is for the client. Skipping a 2% discount to keep the money 20 days longer is like borrowing at about 37% a year, which is why some clients pay early when a discount is offered.

Worked examples

Net 30. An invoice dated Tuesday 20 October 2026 on Net 30 terms is due on Thursday 19 November 2026: 11 days to the end of October, then 19 days of November.

Net 30 EOM. The same invoice on Net 30 EOM terms starts counting on 31 October, so it's due on Monday 30 November 2026, 11 days later.

2/10 net 30. On a $5,000 invoice dated 20 October 2026, the client can pay $4,900 by 30 October (saving $100), or $5,000 by 19 November. Skipping the discount costs the client the equivalent of:

2 ÷ 98 × 365 ÷ (30 − 10) = 37.2% a year

A due date on a weekend. An invoice dated 20 October 2026 on Net 60 terms is due on Saturday 19 December 2026. The calculator tells you when a date falls on a weekend, so you can agree in advance whether the client pays on the Friday before or the Monday after.

Short months. Net 30 from 31 January 2026 is 2 March 2026, because February 2026 has 28 days. In a leap year, such as 2028, the same terms give 1 March.

Payment terms in the UK

GOV.UK says that if you agree a payment date, it must usually be within 60 days for business transactions and 30 days for public authorities. A longer period between businesses is allowed if it's fair to both. If you don't agree a date, the law says payment is late 30 days after the customer gets the invoice or you deliver the goods or service, whichever is later. Source: GOV.UK: late commercial payments, payment terms.

Once a business invoice is late, you can usually claim statutory interest and fixed compensation. The late payment interest calculator works out how much. In the US, Canada and Australia the rules are different and can depend on your state or province, so rely on the terms in your contract.

Tips to get paid on time

  • Agree the terms before you start the work, in your quote or contract, not only on the invoice.
  • Send the invoice as soon as the work is done. The payment days only start once the invoice is dated.
  • Put a reminder in your calendar a few days before the due date, and send a polite note on the day after it's missed.
  • Count working days if you need to. If your contract says "10 working days", use the working days calculator, which skips weekends and public holidays.

What this calculator doesn't include

  • Public holidays. Payment terms in days usually mean calendar days, so holidays still count.
  • Other ways of reading EOM. Some businesses use "EOM" to mean something slightly different. If the wording in your contract isn't clear, ask the client to confirm the date.
  • Interest on late payment. Use the late payment interest calculator for that.

Frequently asked questions

Does Net 30 include weekends?

Usually yes. Net 30 normally means 30 calendar days, so weekends and holidays count. If the terms say "30 working days" or "30 business days", count only working days instead.

Does Net 30 start from the invoice date or when the client gets it?

Usually from the invoice date, but your contract may say otherwise. In the UK, when no date was agreed, the 30 days run from when the customer gets the invoice or the work is delivered, whichever is later.

What does 2/10 net 30 mean?

A 2% discount if the invoice is paid within 10 days; otherwise the full amount is due within 30 days. Enter 2 and 10 in the discount boxes above to see both dates.
