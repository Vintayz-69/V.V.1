What simple interest is

Simple interest is worked out on the starting amount only. Each year earns the same interest, so the total grows in a straight line. With compound interest, interest is added to the balance and then earns interest itself, so the total grows faster over time.

Simple interest is also how our late payment interest calculator works: the invoice amount × the yearly rate ÷ 365, for each day the invoice is late.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the starting amount and choose the currency.
  2. Enter the yearly interest rate as a percentage, such as 5 for 5%.
  3. Enter the time and choose years, months or days.

How it's calculated

Interest = principal × rate × time in years Total = principal + interest Months = months ÷ 12 years Days = days ÷ 365 years Yearly compound (for comparison) = principal × (1 + rate) ^ whole years, plus simple interest for any part year left over

Worked examples

$10,000 at 5% for 3 years.

Interest = 10,000 × 0.05 × 3 = $1,500.00 Total = 10,000 + 1,500 = $11,500.00 Compound = 10,000 × 1.05³ = $11,576.25 ($76.25 more)

£2,500 at 4% for 18 months. 18 months is 1.5 years, so the interest is 2,500 × 0.04 × 1.5 = £150.00, and the total is £2,650.00.

$1,000 at 6% for 90 days. 90 days is 90 ÷ 365 of a year: 1,000 × 0.06 × 90 ÷ 365 = $14.79.

Over a long time the gap grows. $10,000 at 5% reaches $15,000.00 after 10 years with simple interest, but $16,288.95 with yearly compound interest. After 20 years it's $20,000.00 against $26,532.98.

Working backwards

The same formula can be turned round:

Rate = interest ÷ (principal × years) Time = interest ÷ (principal × rate) Principal = interest ÷ (rate × years)

For example, if $10,000 earned $1,500 in 3 years, the rate was 1,500 ÷ (10,000 × 3) = 0.05, which is 5%.

What this calculator doesn't include

  • Deposits or payments during the time. For regular saving, use the compound interest calculator.
  • Tax on interest, and fees.
  • Loan repayments. Most loans and mortgages charge interest on the balance left each month. The mortgage calculator works that out.

Frequently asked questions

What is the simple interest formula?

Interest = principal × rate × time, with the rate as a decimal (5% = 0.05) and the time in years. The total is the principal plus the interest.

Is simple interest better than compound interest?

When you're saving, compound interest earns more at the same rate. When you're paying interest, simple interest costs less. Over one year with yearly compounding, the two are the same.

How do I work out interest for a number of days?

Divide the days by 365 to turn them into years, then use the formula. Choose "Days" above to do this for you.

How is interest on a late invoice worked out?

Our late payment interest calculator works it out as simple interest for each day late, and fills in the UK statutory rate for business debts.
