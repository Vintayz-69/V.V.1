BMI is a screening metric based on the World Health Organization (WHO) international guidelines for adult men and women.

How Body Mass Index is calculated

BMI evaluates the ratio of total body mass to height squared:

Metric: BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [ Height (m) ]² Imperial: BMI = 703 × Weight (lbs) ÷ [ Height (inches) ]²

Frequently asked questions

What are the limitations of the BMI formula?

BMI does not distinguish between lean skeletal muscle mass and adipose fat tissue. Muscular athletes, bodybuilders, and strength trainers may register as "overweight" or "obese" despite having very low body fat percentages.

What is BMI Prime?

BMI Prime is the ratio of your actual BMI to the upper limit of the normal weight threshold (25.0). A BMI Prime below 0.74 indicates underweight, 0.74 to 1.00 is optimal healthy weight, and above 1.00 indicates overweight.

Does BMI apply equally to children and teenagers?

No. Children and teens undergo rapid development and must be evaluated against age-and-sex-specific growth percentiles (CDC/WHO pediatric growth charts), not the adult standard table above.
