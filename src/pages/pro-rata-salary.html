What "pro rata" means

Pro rata means "in proportion". A job advert that says "£30,000 pro rata" gives the salary for working full time. If you work fewer hours, you're paid the same share of that salary as the share of full-time hours you work. Your hourly rate stays the same; you're just paid for fewer hours.

That share is often called your full-time equivalent, or FTE. Three days of a five-day week is 0.6 FTE, so you get 60% of the full-time salary.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the full-time salary from the job advert or your contract.
  2. Enter the full-time hours for the job, such as 37.5 or 40 a week, and the hours you'll actually work.
  3. Starting or leaving part way through the year? Change the weeks to the number of weeks you'll be paid for. Otherwise leave it at 52.

How it's calculated

FTE = your hours ÷ full-time hours Pro rata salary = full-time salary × FTE Paid for the weeks = pro rata salary × weeks ÷ 52 A month = pro rata salary ÷ 12 A week = pro rata salary ÷ 52 Hourly rate = full-time salary ÷ (full-time hours × 52)

Worked examples

Three days a week. Priya is offered a job at £30,000 pro rata, where full time is 37.5 hours. Priya will work three days of 7.5 hours, so 22.5 hours a week.

FTE = 22.5 ÷ 37.5 = 0.6 Pro rata salary = 30,000 × 0.6 = £18,000 a year A month = 18,000 ÷ 12 = £1,500 A week = 18,000 ÷ 52 = £346.15 Hourly rate = 30,000 ÷ (37.5 × 52) = £15.38

30 hours of a 40-hour week. A $52,000 full-time job worked for 30 hours a week is 0.75 FTE: $39,000 a year, $3,250 a month and $750 a week. The hourly rate is $25 either way.

Starting part way through the year. Sam starts a full-time job at £36,000 with 26 weeks of the year left. For those weeks Sam is paid 36,000 × 26 ÷ 52 = £18,000, still £3,000 a month.

Part time and part year together. A $45,000 job, 30 hours of a 37.5-hour week (0.8 FTE), for 39 weeks: the pro rata salary is $36,000 a year, and for 39 weeks that's $27,000.

Part-time workers' rights in the UK

GOV.UK says part-time workers are protected from being treated less favourably than equivalent full-time workers, unless the employer can show an objective reason. Some benefits are applied pro rata. GOV.UK's own example: if full-time workers get a £1,000 Christmas bonus, someone working half the hours should get £500. Read more on GOV.UK: part-time workers' rights.

Paid holiday is worked out in proportion too. Our UK holiday entitlement calculator works out the legal minimum for part-time hours. Rules in the US, Canada and Australia are different, so check your contract or your government's employment website.

Things to check before you accept

  • Which full-time hours the salary is based on. £30,000 for 35 hours and £30,000 for 40 hours give different hourly rates and different pro rata salaries.
  • Paid or unpaid breaks. If lunch is unpaid, count only the hours you're paid for.
  • How starters and leavers are paid. This calculator counts in weeks. Some employers count working days or calendar days instead, which can give a slightly different amount for the first or last month.
  • Bonuses and benefits. Ask which ones are pro rata and which are the same for everyone.

What this calculator doesn't include

  • Tax, pension and other deductions. All figures are gross pay.
  • Overtime or extra hours above your usual week. The overtime calculator covers those.
  • Different pay rates on different days, such as weekend rates.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out a pro rata salary?

Divide your weekly hours by the full-time weekly hours, then multiply the full-time salary by the result. 22.5 hours out of 37.5 is 0.6, so a £30,000 salary becomes £18,000.

Is my hourly rate lower if I work part time?

Not with pro rata pay. The hourly rate is the same as a full-time worker's; you're paid for fewer hours. In the first example above, both work at £15.38 an hour.

I work days, not hours. What do I enter?

Enter the hours of the days you work. If full time is five days of 7.5 hours (37.5) and you work four of them, enter 30. The table above shows the usual day patterns for your salary.

What's my hourly rate from a full-time salary?

Divide the salary by the full-time hours in a year (hours a week × 52). For more ways to split a salary, try the salary to hourly calculator.
