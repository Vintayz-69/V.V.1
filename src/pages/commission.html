Before tax. Commission plans can have caps, clawbacks or rules about when a sale counts; check your own plan.

How to use the commission calculator

  1. Type your sales for the period and your commission rate.
  2. Tiered plan? Tick the box and type the threshold and the higher rate paid on sales above it.
  3. Add base pay for the same period to see your total, and a goal to see the sales you'd need.

How it's calculated

Flat rate commission = sales × rate Tiered commission = (sales up to the threshold × rate 1) + (sales above it × rate 2) Total pay base pay + commission Sales needed goal ÷ rate (tiered: the threshold, plus what's left of the goal ÷ rate 2)

In a tiered plan like this, the higher rate applies only to the part of sales above the threshold, not to all of it. Some plans work differently ("retroactive" tiers pay the higher rate on everything once you pass the threshold), so check yours.

Worked examples

$50,000 of sales at 5% = $2,500 commission with $2,000 base pay = $4,500 total pay Tiered: 5% up to $40,000, 8% above, on $60,000 $40,000 × 5% = $2,000 + $20,000 × 8% = $1,600 = $3,600 (6% of sales) Goal of $3,000 at 5% = $60,000 in sales needed Goal of $3,000, tiered as above = $40,000 + $1,000 ÷ 8% = $52,500

Tiered or retroactive: the same sales, different pay

Two plans can both say "5%, rising to 8% above $40,000" and still pay very different amounts. In a tiered plan (what this calculator uses), 8% applies only to sales above $40,000. In a retroactive plan, once you pass $40,000 the 8% applies to all your sales.

Comparing two job offers with commission

Higher commission and lower base pay can be better or worse, depending on how much you sell. Find the sales level where the two offers pay the same:

Break-even sales = (base pay B − base pay A) ÷ (commission rate A − commission rate B) Offer A: $45,000 base + 3% commission Offer B: $55,000 base + 1% commission Break-even sales = (55,000 − 45,000) ÷ (3% − 1%) = $500,000 a year

If you expect to sell more than $500,000 a year, Offer A pays more. Below that, Offer B does. At $300,000 of sales, for example, A pays $54,000 and B pays $58,000. Be honest about the sales you can really make: past results are a better guide than a recruiter's "typical" figure.

Commission words you might see

  • Quota: the sales target for a period. Many plans pay more, or only start paying commission, once you reach it.
  • On-target earnings (OTE): base pay plus the commission you'd earn if you hit 100% of quota. It's a target, not a promise.
  • Draw against commission: an advance paid each period that later commission pays back. Check whether an unpaid balance has to be repaid if you leave.
  • Clawback (or chargeback): commission taken back if a customer cancels or doesn't pay.
  • Accelerator: a higher rate once you pass quota, which is what the tiered option above models.

The exact meaning is whatever your written plan says. Always read it.

Frequently asked questions

Is commission taxed?

Yes: employees' commission is pay and is taxed like salary, and freelancers' commission is business income. This calculator shows amounts before tax. For take-home pay, see our US and UK take-home pay calculators.

Is commission worked out on sales or on profit?

Either, depending on the plan. If yours pays on gross profit, type the profit as "Sales".

How do I work out my commission rate from a payslip?

Divide the commission by the sales it was paid on, then multiply by 100. $2,500 on $50,000 of sales is 2,500 ÷ 50,000 × 100 = 5%. For a tiered plan, the calculator shows this average as "effective rate".

I'm a freelancer paid a referral fee. Can I use this?

Yes. Type the value of the work you referred as "Sales" and your fee as the rate. Leave base pay at 0. Remember it's business income, so set aside money for tax.

How much do I need to sell to earn a target amount?

Type your goal in the calculator. With a flat rate it's goal ÷ rate: $3,000 at 5% needs $60,000 of sales. With the tiered plan above it's $52,500, because sales above $40,000 earn 8%.
