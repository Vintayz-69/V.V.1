Stamp Duty rates

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is charged in bands, like Income Tax. Each rate applies only to the part of the price inside its band.

Part of the priceStandard rateAdditional property
Up to £125,0000%5%
£125,001 to £250,0002%7%
£250,001 to £925,0005%10%
£925,001 to £1.5 million10%15%
Over £1.5 million12%17%

First-time buyers pay nothing on the first £300,000 and 5% on the part from £300,001 to £500,000. Above £500,000 the relief can't be claimed, so the standard rates apply. Non-UK residents usually pay an extra 2% on every band, on top of any other rates.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the price you're paying for the property.

  3. Tick the box if you're not a UK resident.

How it's calculated

For each band: tax = part of the price in the band × rate Additional property (£40,000 or more): add 5% to every band Not a UK resident: add 2% to every band Stamp Duty = the bands added together

Worked examples

Moving home for £295,000 (GOV.UK's own example):

0% on the first £125,000 = £0 2% on the next £125,000 = £2,500 5% on the final £45,000 = £2,250 Total = £4,750

First-time buyer at £500,000 (GOV.UK's example): 0% on the first £300,000, then 5% on the other £200,000 = £10,000. At £500,001 the relief is lost and the standard rates give £15,000.05, so the price limit matters.

An additional property for £300,000 (GOV.UK's example): 5% on £125,000 (£6,250), 7% on £125,000 (£8,750) and 10% on £50,000 (£5,000) = £20,000.

Paying Stamp Duty

GOV.UK says you must send an SDLT return to HMRC and pay the tax within 14 days of completion. If a solicitor, agent or conveyancer is handling your purchase, they usually file and pay on the day of completion.

Stamp Duty is only one cost of buying a home. To see the monthly repayment on a mortgage, try the mortgage calculator.

What this calculator doesn't include

  • Scotland and Wales. Scotland has Land and Buildings Transaction Tax and Wales has Land Transaction Tax, with different rates.
  • Non-residential or mixed-use property, such as shops, offices or farmland.
  • Special cases, such as shared ownership, buying several homes at once, new leases, companies buying homes, and non-UK residents buying for under £40,000. Use HMRC's own calculator or ask a conveyancer.
  • Exceptions to the higher rates. GOV.UK's guidance on additional properties explains when they don't apply.
  • Other buying costs, such as legal fees and surveys.

Frequently asked questions

When do first-time buyers pay Stamp Duty?

Only on the part of the price over £300,000, at 5%, if the price is £500,000 or less. Above £500,000, first-time buyers pay the same as anyone moving home.

Who counts as a first-time buyer?

GOV.UK says you can claim the relief if the property will be your first home and you and anyone else you're buying with are first-time buyers.

Do I pay the extra 5% on a buy-to-let or second home?

Usually yes, if buying it means you'll own more than one residential property and it costs £40,000 or more. The 5% is added to every band.

Is there Stamp Duty on a £125,000 house?

Not for most buyers: the first £125,000 is taxed at 0%. For an additional property it's 5%, so £6,250.
