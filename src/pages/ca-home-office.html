An estimate for Form T2125, Part 7. The claim can't be more than your net business income before it, and it can't create or increase a loss. Anything left over carries forward.

Can you claim business-use-of-home expenses?

The CRA says self-employed people can claim part of their home costs if the workspace is either:

  • your principal place of business, or
  • used only to earn your business income, and used regularly and continuously to meet clients, customers or patients.

This page is for the self-employed (Form T2125). Employees who work from home claim under different rules.

How the CRA works it out

Business part = workspace area ÷ home area × yearly home expenses If you also use the space personally: Business part = business hours a day ÷ 24 × workspace area ÷ home area × expenses × business days a week ÷ 7 Claim this year = the lesser of (business part + amount carried forward) and net business income before this claim Carry forward = whatever is left

Worked examples from the CRA

Form T2125 example. A workspace of 18 m² in a 180 m² home (10%). Home expenses are $13,300: heat $1,200, electricity $1,000, insurance $650, maintenance $350, mortgage interest $8,000, property taxes $1,800 and water $300. The business part is $1,330. With $150 carried forward from last year, $1,480 is available, and net income of $7,100 is enough to claim all $1,480.

A space used only for business. 20 m² of a 120 m² home, with $6,000 of household expenses: 20 ÷ 120 × $6,000 = $1,000.

A space also used personally. 35 m² of a 100 m² home, used for business 10 hours a day, with $5,800 of expenses: 10 ÷ 24 × 35 ÷ 100 × $5,800 = $845.83. The business runs 5 days a week, so $845.83 × 5 ÷ 7 = $604.16. If it ran all 7 days, the claim would be the full $845.83.

When income is too low. The first example again, but with only $1,000 of net business income. The claim is limited to $1,000, and the other $480 carries forward to next year.

What you can include

  • Everyone: part of heating, electricity, home insurance, water, and maintenance such as cleaning materials.
  • If you rent: part of your rent.
  • If you own: part of your mortgage interest and property taxes.

Don't count the same expense twice: the CRA says amounts you claim here can't also be claimed elsewhere on Form T2125.

A warning about capital cost allowance

You can also claim capital cost allowance (CCA) on the business part of a home you own. The CRA warns that if you do, the capital gain and recapture rules will apply to that part when you sell the home. This calculator doesn't include CCA. An accountant can help you decide whether to claim it.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • Capital cost allowance, as above.
  • Employees working from home, who claim work-space-in-the-home expenses on line 22900 under different rules.
  • Using the space for only part of the year, which reduces the claim further.
  • Quebec provincial tax, handled by Revenu Québec.

Frequently asked questions

Can business-use-of-home expenses create a loss?

No. The CRA says the claim can't be more than your net business income before you deduct it. The rest carries forward to the next year.

How do I work out the business part of my home?

The CRA says to use a reasonable basis, such as the area of the workspace divided by the total area of your home.

I work at my kitchen table. Can I claim?

If the space is your principal place of business, you can claim for the hours and days you use it for business. Tick "I also use this space for personal things" above.
