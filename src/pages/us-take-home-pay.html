How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your yearly salary before tax, as shown in your offer letter or contract.
  2. Choose your filing status. It sets your brackets and standard deduction.
  3. Add pre-tax contributions, such as a traditional 401(k), for the year.
  4. Enter a state income tax rate if your state has one, or leave it at 0.

How Social Security and Medicare (FICA) are taken

  • Social Security: 6.2% of your wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Wages above that aren't taxed for Social Security.
  • Medicare: 1.45% of all your wages, with no limit. An extra 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies to wages over $200,000 if you're single, or $250,000 for married couples filing jointly.

Your employer pays a matching 6.2% and 1.45% on top of your salary. That isn't taken from your pay, so it isn't shown here.

Worked examples

A $75,000 salary. Taylor is single, takes the standard deduction of $16,100, has no 401(k) and lives in a state with no income tax.

Taxable income = 75,000 − 16,100 = $58,900 Federal income tax = 1,240 + 4,560 + 1,870 = $7,670 FICA = 75,000 × 7.65% = $5,737.50 Take-home pay = 75,000 − 7,670 − 5,737.50 = $61,592.50 a year = $5,132.71 a month, or $2,368.94 every two weeks

A $120,000 salary with a 401(k) and state tax. Jordan is single, puts $5,000 into a traditional 401(k) and enters a 5% state rate.

Taxable income = 120,000 − 5,000 − 16,100 = $98,900 Federal income tax = $16,470 FICA = 120,000 × 7.65% = $9,180 State tax = (120,000 − 5,000) × 5% = $5,750 Take-home pay = $83,600 a year, or $6,966.67 a month

Jordan's 401(k) lowers federal and state income tax, but not FICA, which is still worked out on the full $120,000. The $83,600 is pay after tax and after the $5,000 that went into the 401(k).

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • State and local tax in detail. The calculator applies the single state rate you enter to your salary after pre-tax contributions. Many states have brackets and their own deductions, and some cities and counties have income tax too.
  • Tax credits, such as the Child Tax Credit, and itemized deductions.
  • Other deductions from your pay, such as health insurance, an HSA or union dues.
  • Other income, bonuses taxed differently, or a second job.
  • Self-employment. If you freelance, use our freelancer take-home pay calculator, which adds self-employment tax.

Frequently asked questions

How do 401(k) contributions reduce my taxes?

Contributions to a traditional 401(k) or 403(b) come out of your pay before federal income tax, so they lower your taxable income. They don't lower Social Security and Medicare (FICA), which are still taken from your full salary.

What state tax rate should I enter?

Check your state's own tax agency. If your state has brackets, a single rate is only a rough estimate: use a rate a little below your top bracket. If your state doesn't tax wages, enter 0.

Why is my paycheck different from this estimate?

Your employer works out withholding from the W-4 form you filled in and takes out other deductions such as health insurance. Withholding is also an estimate: the final figure is settled when you file your tax return.
