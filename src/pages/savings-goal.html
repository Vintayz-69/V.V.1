How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your goal and anything you've already saved towards it.
  2. Choose how many months you want to reach it in.
  3. Enter the yearly interest rate your savings earn, or 0 if you're keeping the money in cash.
  4. Set up a monthly transfer for the amount shown, ideally on the day you're paid.

How it's calculated

Interest is added monthly at one twelfth of the yearly rate, and you save at the end of each month.

Monthly rate i = yearly rate ÷ 12 Growth factor = (1 + i) ^ months Savings grow to = saved already × growth factor Still needed = goal − what your savings grow to Monthly amount = still needed × i ÷ (growth factor − 1) (or still needed ÷ months if there's no interest)

Worked example

Sara wants $20,000 for a house deposit in 3 years (36 months). She has $2,000 saved, in an account paying 4% a year.

Monthly rate i = 4% ÷ 12 = 0.3333% Growth factor = 1.003333 ^ 36 = 1.12727 Savings grow to = 2,000 × 1.12727 = $2,254.54 Still needed = 20,000 − 2,254.54 = $17,745.46 Monthly amount = 17,745.46 × 0.003333 ÷ 0.12727 = $464.77

Sara needs to save $464.77 a month. She pays in about $16,732 in total and interest adds about $1,268. Without interest she'd need $500 a month.

Saving by paycheck instead of by month

If you're paid weekly or every two weeks, convert the monthly amount so you save the same over a year:

Every two weeks = monthly × 12 ÷ 26 ($464.77 → $214.51) Every week = monthly × 12 ÷ 52 ($464.77 → $107.25)

Don't just halve the monthly figure for every two weeks: there are 26 two-week pay periods in a year, not 24.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include tax on interest?

No. Interest is shown before any tax. In some countries savings interest is taxed above an allowance, and some accounts are tax-free, so the real growth can be a little lower or the same.

What if my savings rate changes?

Run the calculator again with the new rate and the amount you've saved so far. Rates on savings accounts often change, so checking every few months keeps you on track.

How is this different from an emergency fund?

A savings goal is for something you plan to buy. An emergency fund is money kept aside for surprises, such as a lost client or a broken laptop. Our emergency fund calculator works out how much that should be.

How do I see how money grows without a goal?

Use our compound interest calculator, which shows how a starting amount and regular deposits grow year by year.

Should I save or invest for my goal?

That depends on your situation and how soon you need the money. Investments can fall as well as rise. A qualified financial adviser can help you decide.
