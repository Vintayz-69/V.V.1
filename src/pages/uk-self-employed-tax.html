How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your profit: what you earned from self-employment in the tax year, minus allowable business expenses.
  2. Choose where you live. Scotland has its own Income Tax bands; National Insurance is the same across the UK.

How it's calculated

Personal Allowance = £12,570, less £1 for every £2 of profit over £100,000 Taxable income = profit − Personal Allowance Income Tax = taxable income through the 2026 to 2027 bands Class 4 NI = 6% × (profit between £12,570 and £50,270) + 2% × (profit above £50,270) Take-home = profit − Income Tax − Class 4 NI

Worked example

Priya is a self-employed designer in England with £45,000 profit in 2026 to 2027.

Taxable income = 45,000 − 12,570 = 32,430 Income Tax = 32,430 × 20% = 6,486.00 Class 4 NI = (45,000 − 12,570) × 6% = 1,945.80 Take-home = 45,000 − 6,486 − 1,945.80 = £36,568.20

Priya keeps £36,568.20, about £3,047 a month. If she lived in Scotland, her Income Tax would be £6,882.05 and her take-home £36,172.15.

Self Assessment checklist: the 31 January deadline

For the 2026 to 2027 tax year (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027), the main dates are below. Our UK tax deadlines calendar lists every upcoming date and adds them to your calendar. For the year before (2025 to 2026), each date is one year earlier, so the online deadline is 31 January 2027.

  • Register by 5 October 2027 if this is your first tax return, or you didn't need to send one last year. If you register later, HMRC gives you a new deadline, 3 months from the date of its letter or email.
  • Paper return by 31 October 2027, if you send one on paper.
  • Online return by 31 January 2028. Have your income, expenses and any other income (such as a salary or interest) ready.
  • Pay by 31 January 2028: the tax you still owe for 2026 to 2027, plus your first payment on account for the next year, if one applies.
  • Second payment on account by 31 July 2028, if you make them. Our payments on account calculator shows the amounts.

Missing the online deadline means an automatic £100 penalty, even if you owe no tax. After 3 months it's £10 a day (up to £900), with more after 6 and 12 months. Paying late adds 5% of the unpaid tax at 30 days, 6 months and 12 months, plus interest. These are GOV.UK figures; see the penalties page.

If your turnover (before expenses) is over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026, you'll also need to send HMRC quarterly updates under Making Tax Digital from 6 April 2027. Our Making Tax Digital checker shows if and when it applies to you.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • Other income, such as a salary, savings interest, rental income or dividends.
  • Pension contributions, Gift Aid, Marriage Allowance and student loan repayments.
  • Tax years other than 2026 to 2027.

Frequently asked questions

When do I pay this tax?

Through Self Assessment. The tax for 2026 to 2027 is due by 31 January 2028, and you may also have to make payments on account towards it. Our payments on account calculator shows the dates and amounts.

Do I still pay Class 2 National Insurance?

Not usually. Since April 2024, self-employed people with profits of £7,105 or more (for 2026 to 2027) get Class 2 treated as paid. Below that, you can pay it voluntarily to protect your State Pension.

How much should I put aside for tax?

Use the effective rate above for this year's tax. In your first year, also plan for payments on account, which can mean paying up to one and a half years' tax in the same January.

What can I take off as business expenses?

Costs you have only for your business, such as office supplies, business travel, software, insurance and accountancy fees. If something is partly personal, such as a phone, only the business share counts. Our mileage and working from home calculators cover two common ones. GOV.UK lists allowable expenses in full.

What happens if I miss the 31 January deadline?

You get a £100 penalty straight away if your return is late, even if you owe no tax, and more if it's later still. Late payment adds 5% penalties and interest. See the checklist above for the full list.

Should I be a sole trader or set up a limited company?

It depends on your profit and how you'd take money out. Our sole trader vs limited company calculator compares the two using 2026 to 2027 rates. An accountant can help you decide.
