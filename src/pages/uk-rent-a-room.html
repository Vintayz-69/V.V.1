What is the Rent a Room Scheme?

The Rent a Room Scheme lets you earn up to £7,500 a year tax-free from letting furnished accommodation in your only or main home, such as taking in a lodger. GOV.UK says the exemption is automatic if you earn less than the limit. The limit is halved to £3,750 if someone else also gets income from letting accommodation in the same property.

The limit is on your receipts, not your profit. HMRC's helpsheet says receipts include rent and anything your lodger pays you for meals, goods and services such as cleaning or laundry.

If you earn more than the limit

You must send a tax return, and you choose one of two ways to work out your taxable income:

  • Method A (the normal way): your receipts minus your expenses. No Rent a Room relief.
  • Method B (Rent a Room relief): your receipts minus £7,500 (or £3,750). You can't take off any expenses.

The method that gives the lower taxable income leaves less tax. As a rule, Method B wins unless your costs are more than £7,500 (or £3,750).

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter everything your lodger paid you in the tax year, including for meals or cleaning.
  2. Enter your costs of letting, used only for Method A.
  3. Enter your other taxable income so the letting income is taxed at the right rate.
  4. Tick the box if someone else also gets letting income from your home, and enter only your share.

Worked examples

Using 2026 to 2027 Income Tax rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland, with a £30,000 salary unless stated.

HMRC's example (Chris). Receipts of £10,600 (£10,400 rent plus £200 for heating and light) and £9,000 of expenses. Method A taxes £1,600; Method B taxes £3,100. Method A is lower: at 20%, £320 of tax instead of £620.

A lodger at £600 a month. £7,200 a year is under £7,500, so it's tax-free automatically and there's nothing to work out.

A lodger at £800 a month. £9,600 a year with £1,000 of costs. Method A taxes £8,600 (£1,720). Method B taxes £9,600 minus £7,500, so £2,100 (£420). Method B leaves £1,300 less tax.

Sharing the income. A couple share £9,600 equally, so each has £4,800 and a £3,750 limit. For one of them, with £500 of costs: Method A taxes £4,300 (£860); Method B taxes £1,050 (£210).

A higher-rate taxpayer. A £60,000 salary, £12,000 of receipts and £1,500 of costs. Method A taxes £10,500 at 40% (£4,200). Method B taxes £4,500 (£1,800).

In Scotland. The £800-a-month example with a £30,000 salary falls in the 21% intermediate band: Method B's £2,100 means £441 of tax.

Who can use it

  • You let furnished accommodation in your only or main home. You can let as much of your home as you want.
  • GOV.UK says you can opt in at any time if you're a resident landlord, whether or not you own your home, or if you run a bed and breakfast or guest house.
  • You can't use it for homes converted into separate flats, or for accommodation that isn't part of your main home when it's let.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • National Insurance. It works out Income Tax only.
  • Losses and capital allowances. If Method A gives a loss, it shows no tax, and doesn't work out what you can do with the loss.
  • Other property income, such as a buy-to-let flat, or the separate £1,000 property allowance.
  • Lettings that start or stop during the year. HMRC's helpsheet HS223 covers these and other special cases.
  • Your lodger's rights, deposits and agreements. GOV.UK's guide to renting a room in your home explains these.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to tell HMRC about my lodger?

Not for tax, if your receipts are under the limit: GOV.UK says the exemption is automatic. If you earn more than the limit, you must send a tax return.

Does the £7,500 include money for bills and meals?

Yes. HMRC's helpsheet counts payments for meals, goods and services such as cleaning or laundry as receipts, along with the rent.

My partner and I both rent out the room. Do we get £7,500 each?

No. If someone else also gets income from letting accommodation in the same property, each person's limit is £3,750.

Can I claim my costs and the Rent a Room relief?

No. With Method B you can't deduct any expenses or capital allowances. Choose whichever method leaves the lower taxable income.
