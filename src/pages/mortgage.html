How mortgage amortization is calculated

Monthly repayments on a fixed-rate mortgage are calculated using the standard mathematical loan amortization formula:

Monthly Payment (M) = L × [ r(1 + r)^n ] ÷ [ (1 + r)^n - 1 ] Where: L = Loan Amount (Home Purchase Price minus Down Payment) r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12) n = Total Number of Monthly Payments (Loan Term in Years × 12)

How to calculate principal paid on a mortgage

Each monthly payment is split in two. First the lender takes the interest due on what you still owe. Whatever is left of the payment goes to the principal and reduces the balance. Next month the interest is a little smaller, so a little more goes to principal.

Interest this month = balance owed × yearly rate ÷ 12 Principal this month = monthly payment − interest this month New balance = balance owed − principal this month Principal paid so far = amount borrowed − balance owed now

Repeat the three steps for each month. The table above does this for every month of the loan and adds up each year. Amounts are rounded to the cent each month, as lenders do.

Worked example

A home costs $400,000 with an $80,000 down payment, so the loan is $320,000. The rate is 6.5% fixed for 30 years (360 payments).

Monthly rate r = 6.5 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.0054167 Monthly payment = 320,000 × [r(1 + r)^360] ÷ [(1 + r)^360 − 1] = $2,022.62 First payment: Interest = 320,000 × 0.065 ÷ 12 = $1,733.33 Principal = 2,022.62 − 1,733.33 = $289.29 New balance = 320,000 − 289.29 = $319,710.71 Year 1 (12 payments): principal $3,576.76, interest $20,694.68 After 5 years: balance $299,554.97 Principal paid so far = 320,000 − 299,554.97 = $20,445.03

In the first year only about $3,577 of the $24,271 paid goes to the loan itself; the rest is interest. After 5 years, about $20,445 of the principal has been paid off. Over the full 30 years the interest adds up to $408,140.64.

Understanding your monthly housing costs

In addition to repaying the borrowed principal and mortgage interest, homeownership entails recurring escrow costs:

  • Principal: The portion of each payment that directly repays your loan balance and builds equity in the property.
  • Interest: The fee charged by the lender for borrowing capital. In the early years of a 30-year mortgage, the vast majority of each payment goes toward interest.
  • Property Taxes & Insurance: Local municipal or council taxes and buildings insurance are typically bundled into monthly housing escrow.

Frequently asked questions

Why is a 15-year mortgage significantly cheaper overall than a 30-year?

A 15-year loan requires higher monthly repayments, but because the principal balance is paid down twice as quickly, you pay much less interest in total over the life of the loan.

Why does so little of my payment go to principal at first?

Interest is worked out on the balance you still owe. At the start the balance is at its highest, so the interest takes most of the payment. As the balance falls, the interest falls too and more of the same payment goes to principal.

Will my lender's figures match exactly?

They should be close, but they may differ by a few cents or more. Lenders can work out interest daily instead of monthly, round differently, or add fees. Your mortgage statement shows the exact split for each payment.

What is a standard down payment?

A 20% down payment is standard to avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI in the US), although many first-time homebuyer schemes allow deposits between 5% and 10%.
