What net worth means

Your net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. It's a single number that shows where you stand today. It can be negative, for example early in a career with a student loan and few savings. Which way it moves over time often tells you more than the number itself.

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose your currency.
  2. Fill in what you own at today's value. For a home or car, use a realistic selling price, not what you paid.
  3. Fill in what you owe, using the balance left to pay on each debt. Statements and banking apps show these.
  4. Leave any box empty that doesn't apply to you.

How it's calculated

What you own = cash + investments + pensions + home + other property + vehicles + other things What you owe = mortgage + other property loans + car loans + credit cards + student loans + other debts Net worth = what you own − what you owe Debt share = what you owe ÷ what you own × 100 Home equity = home value − mortgage Not counting home = net worth − home equity

Worked examples

A homeowner. Sam has $15,000 in savings, $20,000 in investments, $45,000 in a retirement account, a $300,000 home and a $12,000 car. Sam owes $220,000 on the mortgage, $8,000 on the car, $2,500 on credit cards and $15,000 in student loans.

What you own = 15,000 + 20,000 + 45,000 + 300,000 + 12,000 = $392,000 What you owe = 220,000 + 8,000 + 2,500 + 15,000 = $245,500 Net worth = 392,000 − 245,500 = $146,500 Debt share = 245,500 ÷ 392,000 = 62.6% Home equity = 300,000 − 220,000 = $80,000 Not counting home = 146,500 − 80,000 = $66,500

The home is 76.5% of what Sam owns. Most of Sam's net worth can't be spent without selling or borrowing against the house, which is why the figure "not counting your home" is shown too.

Just starting out. Alex has £3,000 in savings and a car worth £6,000, and owes £4,000 on a credit card and £30,000 in student loans. Net worth is 9,000 − 34,000 = −£25,000.

No debts. £8,000 in savings, £12,000 in investments and £20,000 in a pension, with nothing owed: net worth £40,000, and a debt share of 0%.

Tips for an accurate figure

  • Be realistic about values. A car is worth what it would sell for today, which is often much less than the price paid.
  • Count every debt, including buy now pay later, overdrafts and money owed to family.
  • Pensions are long-term. You may also want to work out your net worth without pensions, as that money often can't be used until later in life.
  • Track it over time. Write the number down every few months. Paying off debt and saving both raise it.

Building savings? The emergency fund calculator and the savings goal calculator help you plan the next step.

What this calculator doesn't include

  • Tax you'd pay if you sold investments or property, and selling costs.
  • Future income, such as salary or a pension you haven't built up yet.
  • Everyday belongings, unless you add them under "other things you own".

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my net worth?

Add up the value of everything you own, add up everything you owe, and take the second from the first. The calculator above does the adding for you.

Should I include my home?

Net worth normally includes your home and its mortgage. Because you can't easily spend the value of your home, this calculator also shows your net worth without it.

Is a negative net worth bad?

It means you owe more than you own right now. This can happen when you have student loans or have just bought something on finance. The direction it moves over time tells you more.

Does net worth include my pension?

Usually yes, at today's value. You can also work it out without pensions, as that money often can't be used until later in life.
