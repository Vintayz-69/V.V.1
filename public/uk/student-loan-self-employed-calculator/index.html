How self-employed people repay a student loan

Employees repay through their pay each month. If you're self-employed, GOV.UK says HMRC works out how much you repay each year from your Self Assessment tax return. It's based on your income for the whole year. If you also have a job, your combined income counts, and anything already taken from your pay is taken off the yearly amount.

Thresholds for 2026 to 2027

PlanYearly thresholdYou repay
Plan 1£26,9009% of income over it
Plan 2£29,3859% of income over it
Plan 4 (Scotland)£33,7959% of income over it
Plan 5£25,0009% of income over it
Postgraduate Loan£21,0006% of income over it

If you have more than one of Plans 1, 2, 4 and 5, GOV.UK says you repay 9% of your income over the lowest of their thresholds, as one repayment. A Postgraduate Loan is repaid at the same time, at 6% over £21,000, on top.

Worked examples

GOV.UK's example. Plan 1, £32,000 of self-employed income plus £10,000 from a job: £42,000 in all. 9% of £15,100 (£42,000 minus £26,900) is £1,359 for the year. The £10,000 job is under the threshold, so nothing has been taken from pay, and all of it is paid through Self Assessment.

Plan 2, self-employed only. £45,000 of profit: 9% of £15,615 is £1,405.35.

A job plus a side business. Plan 2, £35,000 from a job and £10,000 of profit. The yearly repayment on £45,000 is £1,405.35. About £505.35 has been taken from pay (9% of the £5,615 over the threshold), so about £900 is left for Self Assessment.

Plan 5. £30,000 of profit: 9% of £5,000 is £450.

Plan 2 and a Postgraduate Loan. £40,000 of profit: 9% of £10,615 (£955.35) plus 6% of £19,000 (£1,140) is £2,095.35.

Plan 1 and Plan 2. £38,400 of income: 9% over the lower Plan 1 threshold, so 9% of £11,500 is £1,035 a year. That's the £86 a month in GOV.UK's example.

Under the threshold. Plan 2 and £20,000 of profit: nothing to repay this year.

Plan it into your tax savings

Because the repayment is worked out from your tax return after the year ends, it's worth putting money aside for it during the year, as well as for Income Tax and National Insurance. Our self-employed tax calculator works out the tax and NI on the same profit.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • Other income, such as savings interest, dividends or rent. GOV.UK explains when these count.
  • How a repayment is split between plans, and the monthly caps on the plan with the lowest threshold.
  • Repayments that changed during the year, for example from a job that started or ended part-way through. The "already taken from your pay" figure is a yearly estimate.
  • Living abroad. GOV.UK has separate guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Do self-employed people pay student loan repayments monthly?

No. GOV.UK says HMRC works out the amount once a year from your Self Assessment tax return.

Is it based on profit or turnover?

Your income for the year: for self-employment that's your taxable profit, not your sales.

I have a job and a side business. Will I repay twice?

No. Your combined income is used, and what your employer has already taken off your pay is deducted from the yearly amount.
