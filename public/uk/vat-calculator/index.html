How to use this calculator

  1. Choose what you want to do. "Add VAT" turns a price before VAT into the price your customer pays. "Take VAT out" splits a VAT-inclusive total into the price and the VAT.
  2. Pick the rate. Most goods and services are 20%. Some, such as home energy and children's car seats, are 5%. Some, such as most food and children's clothes, are 0%.
  3. Type the amount. The result updates as you type.

How it's calculated

Adding VAT: VAT = price × rate total = price × (1 + rate) Removing VAT: price = total ÷ (1 + rate) VAT = total − price At 20%, the VAT inside a VAT-inclusive total is one-sixth of it (total ÷ 6). At 5%, it is one twenty-first of it (total ÷ 21).

Quoting a day rate once you're VAT-registered

Say your day rate is £400. Once you're registered, your invoice shows £400 plus £80 VAT, a total of £480. The £80 isn't yours: it goes to HMRC on your VAT return, less any VAT you can reclaim on your own business costs. When you work out what you earn, count the £400, not the £480. Always say on quotes whether your price includes VAT, because "£400" and "£400 + VAT" are £80 apart.

If you invoice clients, our invoice generator adds a VAT line, and its page lists what a UK invoice must show.

When you must register for VAT

  • When your total VAT taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000. This is a rolling 12 months, not the tax year.
  • When you expect your turnover to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days alone.
  • Below £90,000 you can choose to register. Once registered, you must charge VAT on your sales and file VAT returns.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • The Flat Rate Scheme, where small businesses pay a fixed percentage of their turnover instead of the difference between VAT charged and paid.
  • Exempt sales (such as most insurance and some education and health services), which aren't the same as zero-rated sales.
  • Sales to customers outside the UK, and the reverse charge for building work and some services.
  • Mixed invoices where different items have different rates. Work those out one line at a time.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out the VAT from a total that includes VAT?

At 20%, divide the total by 6. For example, a £240 invoice includes £40 of VAT, and the price before VAT is £200. Don't take 20% of the total: 20% of £240 is £48, which is too much.

Do freelancers have to charge VAT?

Only once you're VAT-registered. If your turnover is under £90,000 and you haven't chosen to register, don't add VAT to your invoices. Our self-employed tax calculator covers Income Tax and National Insurance, which are separate from VAT.

Is the £90,000 based on profit or sales?

Sales. It's your VAT taxable turnover: the total of everything you sell that isn't exempt from VAT, before taking off any costs.

How do I work out VAT at 5%?

Choose the 5% rate above. To take it out of a total by hand, divide by 21: £105 including VAT at 5% contains £5 of VAT.
