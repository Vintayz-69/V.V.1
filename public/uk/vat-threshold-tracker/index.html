The £90,000 test is a rolling 12 months

GOV.UK says you must register for VAT if your total taxable turnover for the last 12 months goes over £90,000. It's not the tax year or your accounting year. At the end of every month, add up that month and the 11 before it. The first time that total goes over £90,000, the clock starts.

  • Register within 30 days of the end of the month you went over.
  • You're VAT-registered from the first day of the second month after you went over. From then on, you charge VAT on your sales.

GOV.UK's example: your turnover first goes over the threshold on 15 July, so you must register by 30 August, and your effective date of registration is 1 September.

Worked examples

A freelancer whose income is growing (the example above). Sales of £6,000 in November 2025, rising to £11,000 in October 2026. The 12 months to September 2026 only cover 11 months of trading (£87,000). Adding October's £11,000 makes £98,000, so they went over in October 2026. They must register by 30 November 2026, and they're VAT-registered from 1 December 2026.

Steady income under the line. £7,000 every month is £84,000 over 12 months: not over. Next month, there's £13,000 of room (£90,000 minus the last 11 months' £77,000).

Exactly £90,000. £7,500 a month for 12 months is exactly £90,000. That isn't over £90,000, so you don't have to register yet. One more pound in the rolling total would take you over.

One big month. £7,000 a month for 11 months, then £25,000 in July 2026, makes £102,000. Over in July 2026: register by 30 August 2026, registered from 1 September 2026 (the same dates as GOV.UK's example).

The other test: the next 30 days

You must also register if you expect your taxable turnover to go over £90,000 in the next 30 days alone, for example because of one large contract. Then you register by the end of that 30-day period, and your effective date is the date you realised. GOV.UK's example: on 1 May you agree a £100,000 contract paid at the end of May, so you must apply by 30 May, and you're registered from 1 May. This tracker only checks the rolling 12 months.

What counts as taxable turnover

Taxable turnover is the total of everything you sell that isn't exempt from VAT, before costs. Zero-rated sales count towards it. Exempt sales (such as most insurance and some education and health services) don't. If you go over only for a short time, GOV.UK says you can apply for a registration exception, and HMRC decides.

Once you're registered

You add VAT to your prices and send VAT returns. Some small businesses use the Flat Rate Scheme instead of normal VAT returns. Our VAT Flat Rate Scheme calculator compares the two, including the 16.5% rate that applies if you buy very few goods. To add VAT to a price, use the VAT calculator.

What this tracker doesn't cover

  • The next-30-days test, above.
  • Which of your sales are taxable, zero-rated or exempt. Enter taxable sales only.
  • Exceptions and exemptions, which you apply to HMRC for.
  • Businesses not established in the UK, which have different rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is the VAT threshold based on the tax year?

No. It's any 12 months in a row, checked at the end of each month. You can go over in any month of the year.

I went over in October. When do I start charging VAT?

From the first day of the second month after you went over: 1 December. You must register by 30 November.

Is the £90,000 before or after costs?

Before. It's your sales (taxable turnover), not your profit.
