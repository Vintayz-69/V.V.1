Why the salary level matters

As a company director you can pay yourself a salary, dividends, or both. A salary costs the company National Insurance but reduces its Corporation Tax. Dividends don't attract National Insurance, but they're paid out of profit after Corporation Tax. Three salary levels come up most often for 2026 to 2027:

  • £5,000: the employer National Insurance threshold. Below it, the company pays no employer NI.
  • £12,570: the Personal Allowance and the employee NI threshold. Up to here, you pay no Income Tax or employee NI on the salary, but the company pays 15% employer NI on the part above £5,000.
  • £0: all dividends.

Which leaves more depends on the company's Corporation Tax rate. Between £50,000 and £250,000 of profit, marginal relief makes each extra pound of profit cost more than 19%, so a salary that reduces profit saves more Corporation Tax.

The Employment Allowance

The Employment Allowance lets eligible employers reduce their employer National Insurance by up to £10,500 in 2026 to 2027. GOV.UK says a company can't claim it if a director is the only employee paid above the secondary threshold (£5,000 a year). So a one-person company usually can't, but a company that also employs someone else above that level may. Tick the box if yours can.

Worked examples

For 2026 to 2027, England, Wales or Northern Ireland, no Employment Allowance, all remaining profit paid as dividends.

£60,000 of profit. With a £12,570 salary, the company pays £1,135.50 of employer NI, its profit falls to £46,294.50 and its Corporation Tax is £8,795.96 at 19%. You take home £46,091.20. With a £5,000 salary, the company's £55,000 of profit falls in the marginal relief band, Corporation Tax is £10,825, and you take home £45,293.71. With no salary: £44,111.15.

£60,000 with the Employment Allowance. The £1,135.50 of employer NI is covered, so a £12,570 salary leaves £46,732.51.

£30,000 of profit. £12,570 leaves £24,403.45, £5,000 leaves £23,940.65 and £0 leaves £23,092.78.

£100,000 of profit. £12,570 leaves £65,209.62, £5,000 leaves £64,456.96 and £0 leaves £63,605.65.

A salary in between. At £60,000 of profit, a £9,000 salary leaves £45,846.17.

In these examples, £12,570 leaves the most. Your own figures may differ, for example if you have other income or a student loan.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • Other income, such as a job, rent or savings interest, which changes your tax bands.
  • Student loans and pension contributions.
  • Keeping profit in the company instead of paying it all out as dividends.
  • More than one director or shareholder, and associated companies, which change the Corporation Tax limits.
  • Your National Insurance record and State Pension. Check your record on GOV.UK.

Frequently asked questions

Is £12,570 or £5,000 the better director's salary for 2026 to 2027?

In this calculator's examples, £12,570 leaves more take-home, even without the Employment Allowance, because the salary reduces Corporation Tax by more than the employer National Insurance it costs. Try your own profit above, and an accountant can confirm what suits you.

Do I pay National Insurance on a £12,570 salary?

You don't pay employee NI up to £12,570. The company pays 15% employer NI on the part above £5,000 (£1,135.50), unless it can claim the Employment Allowance.

Can a one-person company claim the Employment Allowance?

Usually not. GOV.UK says you can't claim it if a director is the only employee paid above the secondary threshold.
