How it's calculated

CPP = 11.9% × (income up to $74,600 − $3,500) + 8% × (income between $74,600 and $85,000) CPP deduction = employer half + enhanced part of your half + all CPP2 Net income = income − CPP deduction Federal tax = 2026 brackets on net income − 14% × (basic personal amount + base CPP on your half) Take-home = income − CPP − federal tax − provincial estimate

As a self-employed person you pay both the employee and employer shares of CPP. The "base" part of your own share gives a tax credit; the rest is a deduction.

Worked example

Jordan is a self-employed web designer outside Quebec with $60,000 net income in 2026, and estimates provincial tax at 5% of taxable income.

CPP = (60,000 − 3,500) × 11.9% = 6,723.50 CPP deduction = 3,361.75 + 565.00 = 3,926.75 Net income = 60,000 − 3,926.75 = 56,073.25 Federal tax = 56,073.25 × 14% − 14% × (16,452 + 2,796.75) = 5,155.43 Provincial = 56,073.25 × 5% = 2,803.66 Take-home = 60,000 − 6,723.50 − 5,155.43 − 2,803.66 = $45,317.41

Jordan keeps about $45,317, or $3,776 a month. CPP is the biggest single cost at this income, more than federal income tax.

Frequently asked questions

Why is CPP so high when I'm self-employed?

Employees and employers each pay 5.95%. When you're self-employed you're both, so you pay 11.9% (plus 8% CPP2 on earnings between $74,600 and $85,000 in 2026).

Do I have to pay tax in instalments?

If your net tax owing is over $3,000 ($1,800 in Quebec) this year and in either of the two previous years, yes. Our instalments calculator works it out.

Can I get EI as a self-employed person?

Only if you opt in to the EI special benefits program for self-employed people. It covers things like maternity and sickness benefits, not regular job-loss benefits.

When is my tax due if I'm self-employed?

You can file your 2026 return as late as June 15, 2027, but any tax you owe is due by April 30, 2027. See the key dates above.

Do I need to charge GST/HST?

Once your taxable sales pass the CRA's small supplier limit, yes. Our GST/HST calculator explains the limit and shows each province's rate.
