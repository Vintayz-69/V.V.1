How to create and download your freelance invoice

  1. Select your currency and fill in your freelance business details and your client's contact information.
  2. Add your billable line items: set descriptions, hours or quantities, and agreed rates. Add any optional discount or sales tax / VAT.
  3. Include clear payment details: your bank routing/account numbers, IBAN, BSB or payment links, along with due dates.
  4. Preview live and click “Download PDF”: A professional, print-ready PDF is rendered instantly on your device with no watermarks and no sign-up required.

How the invoice total is calculated

Line Item Total = Quantity × Unit Rate Subtotal = Sum of all Line Items Discount Amount = Subtotal × (Discount % ÷ 100) Taxable Amount = Subtotal − Discount Amount Tax (VAT / GST) = Taxable Amount × (Tax % ÷ 100) Total Amount Due = Taxable Amount + Tax

Worked example

Sarah Chen completed a brand identity project for $2,500, 25 hours of UI design at $85/hour ($2,125), and design system guidelines for $750. Her subtotal was $5,375.00. Sarah gave a 5% repeat client discount ($268.75), reducing the taxable amount to $5,106.25. With 0% sales tax applied, her total invoice due was $5,106.25.

What an invoice must show in each country

The rules are different in each country. Here's what each government says, in short. The generator has boxes for the invoice number, dates, both parties' details, line items, tax and payment notes. Items without their own box, such as a supply date, a GST/HST number or an ABN, can go in your business details line or the notes. Check every invoice before you send it.

United Kingdom

GOV.UK says every invoice must include:

  • a unique identification number
  • your name (or company name), address and contact information
  • the name and address of the customer you're invoicing
  • a clear description of what you're charging for
  • the date the goods or service were provided (supply date) and the date of the invoice
  • the amounts being charged, the VAT amount if applicable, and the total amount owed

Sole traders must show their own name and any business name they use, plus an address where legal documents can be delivered if they use a business name. Limited companies must use their full name as it appears on the certificate of incorporation. If you and your customer are both VAT registered, you must use a VAT invoice, which needs more details. Source: GOV.UK: what invoices must include.

United States

The IRS doesn't publish a list of what a freelancer's invoice must show. It treats invoices as records that support the income you report, so keep a copy of every invoice you send. Many freelancers include an invoice number, dates, both parties' details, what the work was, the amount and the payment terms. Sales tax rules are set by each state. Source: IRS: what kind of records should I keep.

Canada

If you're registered for GST/HST, your business customers need certain details on your invoice to claim input tax credits. The CRA's requirements depend on the total sale:

Total saleInvoice needs
Under $100Your business or trading name, the invoice date, and the total amount
$100 to $499.99All of the above, plus your GST/HST registration number and the GST/HST charged (or a note that the price includes it)
$500 or moreAll of the above, plus the buyer's name, a brief description of the services, and the terms of payment

Source: CRA: input tax credits.

Australia

If you're registered for GST, your invoice to a business customer should be a tax invoice. The Australian Government's business.gov.au says it must include the words "tax invoice", your business name, your ABN, the date you issue it, a short description of what you sold (with quantity and price) and the GST amount, if any. The ATO adds that for sales of $1,000 or more, it must also show the buyer's identity or ABN. Choose "Tax invoice" in the Title box above to print those words on your PDF. If you're not registered for GST, business.gov.au says there's no law setting out what a regular invoice must contain; label it "invoice", not "tax invoice". Sources: business.gov.au: how to invoice and ATO: tax invoices.

Frequently asked questions

Are my client invoices saved or stored online?

No. Your invoice is created and turned into a PDF entirely inside your web browser. Your details, your client's details, bank details and amounts are never sent to us. Once you close the page, the information is gone unless you click Save draft to download a backup file to your own computer.

Can I add VAT, GST, or local sales tax?

Yes. Enter the tax rate that applies to your invoice in the Tax % box. The generator applies it after any discount and shows it as a separate line on the PDF. Whether you must charge tax, and at what rate, depends on your country, your registration and your client, so check with your tax authority or an accountant. For Canada and Australia, our GST/HST calculator and GST calculator show the current rates and link to the official sources.

What payment terms should freelancers include?

Many freelancers use "Net 14" (due within 14 days) or "Net 30" (due within 30 days), but agree the terms with your client before you start work. A clear due date and clear payment instructions on the invoice make it easier for clients to pay on time. If a business client in the UK pays late, our late payment interest calculator shows what you can claim.

Can I re-open and edit a previous invoice later?

Yes! Click Save draft to download a lightweight .json file to your device. Next time you visit this page, click Open draft to pick that file up right where you left off.
