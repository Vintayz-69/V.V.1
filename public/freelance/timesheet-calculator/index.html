How to use this calculator

  1. Type your start and finish times for each day you worked. Use the 24-hour clock (17:30) or am and pm (5:30pm).
  2. Add your unpaid break for each day, in minutes. Leave it empty if you didn't take one, or if your breaks are paid.
  3. Enter your hourly rate to see your pay. If overtime starts after a set number of hours in the week, add that and the overtime rate.
  4. Copy the timesheet to paste it into an email, a spreadsheet or an invoice.

How it's calculated

Hours for a day = finish − start − break (add 24 hours if the finish is before the start) Hours for a week = the days added together Decimal hours = minutes ÷ 60 (7 h 30 min = 7.5 hours) Pay = normal hours × rate + overtime hours × rate × overtime rate

Worked examples

9:00 to 17:30 with a 30-minute break = 8 h 00 min = 8.00 hours Five days like that at $25 an hour = 40.00 hours → $1,000.00 Night shift 22:00 to 06:00, 30-minute break = 7 h 30 min = 7.50 hours 8:15 to 16:45, 45-minute break = 7 h 45 min = 7.75 hours 45 hours at £18, overtime after 40 at 1.5× = 40 × 18 + 5 × 27 = £855.00

Common timesheet mistakes

  • Writing 7.30 for seven and a half hours. In decimal hours that's 7.5. Billing 7.30 hours loses 12 minutes of pay each time.
  • Forgetting shifts that cross midnight. 22:00 to 06:00 is 8 hours, not −16. The calculator adds a day when the finish is earlier than the start.
  • Taking off paid breaks. Only take off breaks you aren't paid for. Your contract or employer's policy says which ones are paid.
  • Rounding each day differently. If your employer or client rounds time (for example to the nearest 15 minutes), use the same rule every day and check it's written down.

Frequently asked questions

Why is 7 hours 30 minutes shown as 7.5?

Pay is worked out on decimal hours, where 30 minutes is half an hour (0.5), not 0.30. Both are shown, so you can use whichever your client or employer asks for.

How do I turn decimal hours back into minutes?

Multiply the part after the decimal point by 60. 7.6 hours is 7 hours and 0.6 × 60 = 36 minutes, so 7 h 36 min.

Can I use this for overtime?

Yes. Enter the weekly hours after which overtime starts, such as 40, and the overtime rate, such as 1.5. Hours above the limit are paid at the higher rate. For more overtime options, such as double time, use our overtime calculator.

Is my timesheet saved?

No. Nothing is stored on your device or sent to us, so the times are gone when you close the page. Use "Copy timesheet" to keep a copy in your own email, notes or spreadsheet.

I'm a freelancer. How do I bill these hours?

Put the total hours and your rate into our invoice generator. To check your rate covers tax, time off and unpaid admin, use the hourly rate calculator.
