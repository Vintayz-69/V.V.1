ROAS in an ad platform only counts revenue. This calculator also takes off product costs and other expenses to show profit.

How ROAS and break-even ROAS are calculated

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) = Ad Revenue ÷ Ad Spend Break-Even ROAS = 1 ÷ [ 1 - (COGS % ÷ 100) ] Net Campaign Profit = Revenue - Product COGS - Ad Spend - Other Expenses Cost per Acquisition = Ad Spend ÷ Orders Average Order Value = Revenue ÷ Orders

Break-even ROAS is the ROAS at which the profit from your sales exactly pays for the ads. It only uses product costs (COGS), so if you also have other campaign costs, you need a ROAS a little above it to make a profit.

Worked example

A shop spends $2,000 on ads and gets 100 orders worth $8,000. Its products cost 40% of the selling price, and there are $500 of other costs such as shipping and fees.

ROAS = 8,000 ÷ 2,000 = 4.0x Product costs = 8,000 × 40% = $3,200 Net profit = 8,000 − 3,200 − 2,000 − 500 = $2,300 (28.8% of revenue) Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = 1.67x Cost per order = 2,000 ÷ 100 = $20 Average order = 8,000 ÷ 100 = $80

Why a high ROAS can still lose money

A ROAS of 2.5x sounds good: $2.50 back for every $1 spent. But if your products cost 65% of their selling price, your break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ (1 − 0.65) = 2.86x. Spending $1,000 to make $2,500 in sales then leaves you with a $125 loss, because the products cost $1,625. Always compare your ROAS with your break-even ROAS, not with a general target.

What this calculator doesn't include

  • Repeat customers. A campaign that loses a little on the first order can still be worth it if customers come back. Our customer lifetime value calculator estimates what a customer is worth over time.
  • Returns and refunds. Take them out of the revenue figure before you enter it.
  • Tax. Enter revenue without sales tax, VAT or GST, and remember the profit shown is before income tax.

Frequently asked questions

What is a "good" ROAS?

There isn't one number that suits every business. A good ROAS is one above your own break-even ROAS, which depends on your product costs. Products with high margins can make a profit at a much lower ROAS than products with thin margins.

What is the difference between ROAS and ROI?

ROAS compares revenue with ad spend only. ROI (return on investment) compares profit with everything you spent, including product costs, staff and overheads. A campaign can have a high ROAS and a negative ROI.

Why doesn't my ad platform's ROAS match this?

Ad platforms count the sales they can link to an ad, using their own rules for which clicks or views get the credit. Your shop's own sales records may show a different total. Use the figure you trust most, and use the same one each time so you can compare campaigns fairly.
