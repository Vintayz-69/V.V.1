How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your expected net profit for 2026: income minus business expenses.
  2. Choose your filing status.
  3. Optionally add a state tax rate. State rules vary a lot, so this is a simple flat estimate. Check your own state's department of revenue for its rates.

How it's calculated

Self-employment tax = see our self-employment tax calculator Adjusted gross income = profit − half of self-employment tax Taxable (before QBI) = AGI − standard deduction QBI deduction = 20% × the smaller of (profit − half SE tax) and taxable income (only below the 2026 threshold) Taxable income = taxable before QBI − QBI deduction Federal income tax = 2026 tax brackets applied to taxable income Take-home = profit − income tax − SE tax − Additional Medicare − state tax

2026 standard deduction: $16,100 single or married filing separately, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household.

Worked example

Jamie is single with $80,000 net profit and lives in a state with no income tax.

Self-employment tax = 11,303.64 (half = 5,651.82) AGI = 80,000 − 5,651.82 = 74,348.18 Taxable before QBI = 74,348.18 − 16,100 = 58,248.18 QBI deduction = 20% × 58,248.18 = 11,649.64 Taxable income = 58,248.18 − 11,649.64 = 46,598.54 Income tax = 1,240 + 12% × (46,598.54 − 12,400) = 5,343.83 Total tax = 11,303.64 + 5,343.83 = 16,647.47 Take-home = 80,000 − 16,647.47 = $63,352.53

Jamie keeps $63,352.53, about $5,279 a month. Total federal tax is 20.8% of profit, even though Jamie is in the 12% bracket, because self-employment tax applies from the first dollar.

Turning the rate into a set-aside habit

Once you know your share, apply it to every payment as it arrives. If your effective rate is about 21% federal and your state adds about 4%, set aside about 25% of each payment:

Client pays you $2,000 Set aside for tax (25%) $500 Yours to spend $1,500

Keep the set-aside money in a separate savings account, then use it for your quarterly estimated tax payments. Re-run this calculator every few months as your income changes.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • Other income (W-2 wages, investments), itemized deductions and tax credits such as the Child Tax Credit.
  • The QBI deduction above the 2026 threshold, and the self-employed health insurance and retirement deductions.
  • Exact state and local taxes. The state figure is a simple flat estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I set aside for taxes as a freelancer?

Use the share of profit this calculator shows, plus your state tax. There's no single right percentage: in the table above it runs from about 19% to 25% federal, depending on profit and filing status. See the set-aside example above.

What is the QBI deduction?

The qualified business income deduction lets many self-employed people deduct up to 20% of their business income. Below the 2026 threshold ($201,750, or $403,500 married filing jointly) the calculation is simple, and this calculator includes it.

Why is my effective rate higher than my tax bracket?

Your bracket is for income tax only. Self-employment tax of about 15.3% is added on top, from your first dollar of profit.

Do business expenses lower my tax?

Yes. Enter your profit after expenses. Each dollar of legitimate expenses lowers both self-employment tax and income tax. Our mileage and home office calculators cover two common deductions.

How is this different from the W-2 take-home calculator?

Employees pay only half of Social Security and Medicare and have tax withheld from each paycheck. If you're on a salary, use our US take-home pay calculator for employees.
