How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your expected 2026 net profit and filing status.
  2. Add any tax already withheld, for example from a part-time job.
  3. If you filed a 2025 return, add your total 2025 tax and AGI. Basing payments on last year's tax is often the easiest safe harbor.

How it's calculated

This year's tax = federal income tax + self-employment tax + Additional Medicare Payments required? = yes if (this year's tax − withholding) is $1,000 or more Safe harbor = the smaller of: 90% of this year's tax, or 100% of your 2025 tax (110% if 2025 AGI was over $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately) Quarterly payment = (safe harbor − withholding) ÷ 4

Worked example

Jamie is single and expects $80,000 net profit in 2026, with no other income. Jamie's total 2025 tax was $12,000 and 2025 AGI was under $150,000.

2026 tax (estimate) = $16,647.47 90% of 2026 tax = 14,982.72 100% of 2025 tax = 12,000.00 ← smaller, so this is the safe harbor Quarterly payment = 12,000 ÷ 4 = $3,000 Full quarterly = 16,647.47 ÷ 4 = $4,161.87

Paying $3,000 each quarter avoids a penalty; Jamie would then owe about $4,647 more when filing. Without a 2025 return to go on, the safe harbor would be 90% of this year's tax: $3,745.68 a quarter.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • State estimated tax, which most states with an income tax also require.
  • The annualized income method for uneven income, and farmers and fishermen's special rules.
  • Credits, itemized deductions and other income. See our take-home pay calculator for how the federal estimate is built.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay quarterly estimated taxes?

Usually yes, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file after subtracting withholding and credits. Most full-time freelancers do.

What happens if I miss a payment?

The IRS can charge an underpayment penalty, worked out like interest on the amount that was late. Paying as soon as you can reduces it.

How do I pay?

Online through IRS Direct Pay or your IRS Online Account, through EFTPS, or by mailing a payment voucher from Form 1040-ES.

Sources (tax year 2026): 2026 Form 1040-ES, IRS estimated taxes, Rev. Proc. 2025-32.
