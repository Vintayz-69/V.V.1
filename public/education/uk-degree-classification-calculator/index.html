The usual boundaries

Degree classOverall mark
First-class honours (1st)70 and above
Upper second-class honours (2:1)60 to 69
Lower second-class honours (2:2)50 to 59
Third-class honours (3rd)40 to 49

How it's calculated

Overall mark = second-year average × second-year share + final-year average × final-year share Needed in final year = (class boundary − second-year average × second-year share) ÷ final-year share

Worked examples

62 in year 2, 68 in year 3, weighted 1:2: (62 × 1 + 68 × 2) ÷ 3 = 66.0 → 2:1 For a First: (70 × 3 − 62) ÷ 2 = 74.0 needed in year 3 58 in year 2, 72 in year 3, weighted 40:60: 58 × 0.4 + 72 × 0.6 = 23.2 + 43.2 = 66.4 → 2:1 71 in the final year only = 71.0 → First

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • Your university's own rules for borderline marks (for example, 68 or 69), such as looking at how many credits you got at the higher class.
  • Rules that drop your lowest module marks, or weight modules by credits within a year. Work out each year's average first, then enter it here.
  • Integrated master's degrees with a fourth year, and degrees from outside the UK.

Each mark below 60 in the second year needs half a mark more in the final year. Your university's own rules decide the weighting and any rounding or borderline policy, so check your course handbook.

Frequently asked questions

Does first year count?

At most UK universities, first year only needs a pass and doesn't count towards your degree class. Some courses are different, so check your course handbook or degree regulations.

Where do I find my university's weighting?

Search your university's website for "degree classification" or "academic regulations", or look in your course handbook. It's often given as a ratio such as 1:2 or 40:60.

What if I study in the US?

US colleges use grade point averages instead. Use our GPA calculator.
